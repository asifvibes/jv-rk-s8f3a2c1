ReturnKoto? star ratings: every fund, every number

The rating table behind the stars on every fund page: all 4 tracked funds, their four sub-scores, composite score and the arithmetic that turns a score into stars. Experimental and a work in progress. Full method on the methodology page.

  • What the stars measure: each fund’s past return against two yardsticks, the 91-day T-bill (10.9%/yr) and the DSEX index (-3.2%/yr) over the 36 months to Jul 2026, weighed with how bumpy the ride was and how far it fell. Every fund is measured over the same months.
  • What they do not mean: a forecast, or advice to buy or sell.
  • Held at 3 stars: a fund whose return did not beat both yardsticks (income funds: the T-bill) cannot go above 3 stars, whatever its score. Each row says which it beat.

How a score becomes stars

Star bands for each reference level. Bands apply to the unrounded score, so a score printed exactly on a boundary can sit on either side of it.
StarsGrowth, Balanced, Shariah (level 56)Income (level 72)
5 stars74 or more90 or more
4 stars62 to under 7478 to under 90
3 starsabove 50, under 62above 66, under 78
2 starsabove 38, up to 50above 54, up to 66
1 star38 or below54 or below

Every fund is measured over the exact same months: a 3-year rating on the last 36 months (the main rating above) and a 5-year rating on the last 60 months for funds old enough. Nothing is averaged over each fund’s own lifetime, so a fund launched after a crash is never compared on friendlier years than its peers. Four sub-scores are measured inside the window, each 0 to 100. Return is annualized total return against a benchmark measured over the same window: for growth and balanced funds the midpoint of the DSEX index return (-3.2%/yr over the current 3-year window) and the 91-day T-bill average (10.9%), a hurdle of 3.9%; for income funds the T-bill average alone. Matching the benchmark scores 50, and each percentage point above or below moves the score 5 points. Stability is downside deviation, only losing months count: 1% scores 100, 12% scores 0. Drawdown is the worst peak-to-trough fall (0% scores 100, a 30% fall scores 0) and Consistency is the share of 12-month holding periods that ended in profit. The score is 0.40×Return + 0.20×Stability + 0.20×Drawdown + 0.20×Consistency, and stars come from the fixed bands above, not a curve. One guardrail: stars above 3 require the fund’s return to beat both yardsticks over the window, the 91-day T-bill average and, for growth and balanced funds, the DSEX return itself. A calm NAV alone can never look premium.

Every anchor is pinned to an observable market fact. The T-bill averages come from Bangladesh Bank auction results as reported in the financial press (the 5-year average is approximate, since 2021-22 auction prints are sparse). The DSEX window returns come from our published dsex.json series; DSEX is a price-only index that excludes dividends, which makes the market yardstick slightly easier to beat, and we say so rather than hide it. The downside-deviation cap of 12% sits just above the DSEX’s own 9.9%, and the 30% drawdown cap sits against the DSEX’s worst fall of 36.7%. One honest caveat: income funds hold bonds often priced at amortized cost, which makes their NAVs look smoother than reality; that is one reason return carries the largest weight and the gate exists. Check one by hand

Take IDLC Growth Fund (IGF). Over the current 3-year window it scores Return 74, Stability 54, Drawdown 71 and Consistency 92 (all in ratings.json). So: 0.40×74 + 0.20×54 + 0.20×71 + 0.20×92 = 73.0, against the published score of 73.1. Sub-scores are printed rounded while the score is computed before rounding, so hand math can differ by a few tenths. It is a growth fund, its reference level is 56, and 73.1 sits +17.1 points versus that level, which lands in the 4-star band, held at 3 because over the window it did not beat the 91-day T-bill average (10.9%/yr). Every rating on this page can be rebuilt the same way.

Ratings are recomputed after every NAV collection, but both windows still end at the last completed calendar month covered by the fund data and the DSEX series, so stars move only when a month closes; calculators use daily prints immediately. Ratings describe past performance only; they do not predict the future. The rank shown under each score is context, never an input: stars come from the fixed bands alone. Reference levels and anchors are reviewed once a year in July under the procedure on the methodology page. Raw inputs for every fund are also on its own page. For learning and research.
