Two sides of the coin

Same fund. Two honest answers.

Both are right; they answer different questions. On the left, every dividend is taken as cash the way many investors actually use them, which is VIPB’s since-inception money-weighted return (XIRR). On the right, every dividend is reinvested to buy more units, the total return of the fund itself.

VIPB method+8.05%This is the official since-inception XIRR that VIPB Asset Management Company Limited (VIPB AMCL) uses to share this fund’s performance: one unit at face value, each cash dividend taken out (not reinvested), and today’s NAV as the final payoff./yr · dividends taken as cash · since launch · to 6 Aug 2026
ReturnKoto? METHOD+7.56%/yr · dividends reinvested · since launch · dated pay dates

৳A ৳1,00,000 investment held over since launch would be worth about ৳2,07,940 with dividends reinvested, or ৳1,88,500 taking every dividend as cash. Reinvesting compounds, so it ends higher; the gap (about ৳19,440) is the cost of not reinvesting.

Why can both numbers be right? Seeing two different percentages for the same fund is normal, not a mistake. VIPB’s figure assumes each payout stayed in your hand (a cash-out, money-weighted XIRR). ReturnKoto? METHOD assumes every dividend went back into the fund (a reinvested, time-weighted total return). Both figures include the fund’s stock dividends and unit conversions, so nothing is left out; the difference is only whether dividends are reinvested or taken as cash, plus payout timing, how long you measure and the end date, any of which can tip either side higher. The Taken as cash toggle elsewhere on ReturnKoto? is our own time-weighted cash path for whatever period you pick; it is not the same rate as VIPB’s since-inception money-weighted XIRR on the left, even though both treat dividends as cash. VIPB has reviewed the fund data on this page, and neither figure is an error.

Questions about VIPB Accelerated Income Unit Fund

What kind of fund is VIPB Accelerated Income Unit Fund?
VIPB Accelerated Income Unit Fund is an open-end income mutual fund in Bangladesh, managed by VIPB Asset Management Company Limited (VIPB AMCL). You buy and redeem units at its published net asset value (NAV), and it has no fixed maturity.

What is the historical return of VIPB Accelerated Income Unit Fund?
Over roughly 10.2 years to 6 Aug 2026, VIPB Accelerated Income Unit Fund returned about +7.56% per year, as a total return with dividends reinvested. The figure changes with the period you choose, and past performance does not guarantee future results.

What are the fees for VIPB Accelerated Income Unit Fund?
VIPB Accelerated Income Unit Fund charges a management fee of 2.50% down to 1.00% per year (tiered by fund size). Its published NAV, and so every return shown, is already net of this fee.

Does VIPB Accelerated Income Unit Fund have an exit load?
Buy/sell spread up to 3% of face value.

What is the minimum investment for VIPB Accelerated Income Unit Fund?
VIPB Accelerated Income Unit Fund has a one-time minimum of BDT 20,000 individual / BDT 50,000 institution and a monthly SIP from BDT 2,000/month.

Is VIPB Accelerated Income Unit Fund Shariah-compliant?
No. VIPB Accelerated Income Unit Fund is a conventional open-end fund, not Shariah-certified. ReturnKoto? has a one-tap filter to show only Shariah funds.
