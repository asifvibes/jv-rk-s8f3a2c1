## Reading the comparison honestly

The averages above invite a conclusion they cannot really support. One window is not enough to say a category is better, and the periods when income funds look strong are usually periods when interest rates are high and equities are flat. Those conditions reverse.

What the split does support is a fairer comparison. If you are choosing between two funds, choose within a type first. A growth fund beating an income fund tells you almost nothing about either manager.

The second use is expectation setting. If you cannot hold through the drop that a growth fund's spread implies, the honest answer is that you want a balanced or income fund, and a higher headline number elsewhere is not worth the sleep.

## How type is assigned

Each fund's category follows its own stated mandate in its prospectus, not our judgment of what it holds. Where a fund calls itself balanced, it appears under balanced, even if its current mix leans one way. The holdings panel on each fund page shows the actual mix as of its latest AMC portfolio statement, which is the better guide to what it owns today.
